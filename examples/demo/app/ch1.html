SECTION 1

Income and spending

Money arrives as income and leaves as three kinds of cost; what remains is the surplus.

Textbook open at p.2
TermMeaning
Fixed costsSame every month: rent, loan payments, insurance premiums.
Variable costsChange with your choices: groceries, fuel, entertainment.
Irregular costsArrive a few times a year: repairs, annual subscriptions, gifts. Divide the yearly total by twelve and set it aside monthly.
SurplusWhat is left after all three; negative means the plan spends more than it earns.
Coverage checklist: what the source says here (4 items)
  • Budget = plan for where income goes
  • Fixed, variable, irregular costs
  • Irregular costs cause surprises: yearly total / 12
  • Surplus and negative surplus
SECTION 2

The three-bucket rule of thumb

Split after-tax income into needs, wants and savings; 50/30/20 is a first draft, not a law.

Textbook open at p.2
TermMeaning
NeedsWhat you must pay to live and work.
WantsWhat you choose.
SavingsEmergency fund, retirement, goals, extra debt payments.
Beyond the book – verify: Other splits exist (for example 60/20/20); pick the one that fits your costs.
Coverage checklist: what the source says here (4 items)
  • Three buckets
  • 50/30/20 as a common start
  • Rule of thumb, not a law
  • Expensive cities push needs above half

Tracking and reviewing

A budget is a four-step loop; zero-based budgeting gives every unit of income a job.

SECTION 4

Quiz and self-check

Scenario questions, scored. Pick the BEST answer.

Chapter 1 quizScore 0 / 3

1. Maya earns 3,000 after tax. Rent and loan payments are 1,400 and groceries are about 500. A 600 car service arrives once a year. What should she do first about the car bill?

Irregular costs are divided by twelve and set aside monthly so they never become a surprise.

2. A household in a high-rent city spends 62 percent of income on needs. Which statement about the 50/30/20 split is the BEST?

It is a starting draft, not a law; needs can exceed half and the other buckets shrink.

3. Two months in a row, the dining-out category ended 80 over plan. What does the loop suggest?

A category wrong twice needs a new number, not more willpower.
What is the surplus?

Income left after fixed, variable and irregular costs; negative means the plan overspends.

What is zero-based budgeting?

Every unit of income gets a job so income minus planned spending equals zero; savings count as a job.
