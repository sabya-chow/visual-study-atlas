# Quiz styles (chosen once in the questionnaire, applied to every chapter)

Ask the style at the start; do not mix styles unless the user chose "mixed". Write every question from the source text only. Put the correct answer in a different position from question to question. Each question needs one explanation that says why the best answer is best and, where useful, why a tempting option fails.

## 1. Certification-exam style (scenario, best answer)
- A two-to-three-line scenario with concrete details, then "Which is the BEST ...?" or "What should be done FIRST?".
- Four options that are all plausible to someone who half-knows the topic; one is clearly best by the source's logic.
- Test judgment: ordering, priority, "most appropriate", not just definitions.
- Explanation names the principle used and why the closest distractor is weaker.
Example (neutral): "A household earns 3,000 a month and has a 600 car service due once a year. What should they do FIRST about the bill?" Options: ignore it until it arrives / set aside 50 a month / cut groceries to zero / raise savings to 30 percent. Best: set aside 50 a month, because irregular costs are spread over twelve months.

## 2. Plain recall
- "What is X?" or "Which term means Y?", four options, short explanation quoting the definition.
- Good for vocabulary-heavy chapters; keep distractors from the same chapter so they are real alternatives.

## 3. True or false with a reason
- A claim; options: True because ..., True but ..., False because ..., False but ... (two plausible reasons each way), explanation names the rule.

## 4. Mixed
- Per chapter: about half scenario, a third recall, the rest true/false.

## Sizing
- About 5 to 8 questions per chapter for a short chapter, 10 to 15 for a long one, covering every section at least once.
- Add `ref="#chN-sM"` to link a question to the section to review.
